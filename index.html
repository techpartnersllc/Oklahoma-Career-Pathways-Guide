A living index of IT, data center, cybersecurity, AI/data, software, and skilled-trades career pathways in Tulsa and across Oklahoma — free and sponsored training, apprenticeships, scholarships, and where to find real openings.

Career Tracks by Role Type

Data Center Operations

Data centers are the physical backbone of AI and cloud infrastructure. Oklahoma is adding significant capacity through Meta (East Tulsa) and Google's Pryor, Stillwater, and newly confirmed Project Clydesdale 506-acre campus near Owasso in Tulsa County, built with Beale Infrastructure (also confirmed by the Tulsa Regional Chamber). These roles typically do not require a four-year degree and offer competitive starting salaries.

Typical Entry Salaries in Oklahoma (2026)

LevelSalary Range
Entry-Level Technician$52,900 – $64,100
Mid-Career (5-10 yrs)$64,100 – $76,300
Senior Specialist$76,300+

Source: DC Geeks Data Center Salary Report 2026

Key Roles

  • Data Center Technician I/II — Hardware installation, rack/stack, break-fix, monitoring
  • Critical Facilities Technician — MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing) operations and maintenance
  • Data Center Operations Manager — Shift supervision, incident management, vendor coordination
  • Commissioning Agent — Testing and verification of electrical/mechanical systems during buildout

How to Get In

  • Tulsa Tech programs in IT, Network Support, and Enterprise Technologies — entry point for tech ops roles
  • America's Workforce Academy (Meta/AWA) — CBRE's free Fiber Technician and Construction Ready pathways make participants workforce-ready in four weeks for data-center construction
  • Electrical Training Alliance — apprenticeship path into critical facilities roles
New · 2026–2027
Beale Infrastructure + Northeast Tech — Claremore Data Center Workforce Pipeline New

Beale Infrastructure committed $3.5 million to Northeast Tech's Claremore campus, about 30 minutes from Tulsa, for classrooms, industry-aligned curriculum, and hands-on certifications in data-center operations, cybersecurity, and IT. Funding is planned in two installments across 2026–2027.

Information Technology (IT) Infrastructure

These roles sit at the intersection of networking, systems, and security. Strong demand from both enterprise employers and data center operators in Oklahoma.

Key Roles

  • Network Engineer / Network Administrator — Routing, switching, LAN/WAN, SD-WAN
  • Systems Administrator — Windows/Linux server management, virtualization (VMware, Hyper-V)
  • Cloud Engineer (Azure / AWS / GCP) — Cloud architecture, IaC, hybrid environments
  • DevOps / Platform Engineer — CI/CD pipelines, containerization (Docker/Kubernetes), automation
  • IT Support Technician / Help Desk — Tier 1-3 support, endpoint management, ITSM

Typical Certifications That Open Doors

  • CompTIA A+, Network+, Security+
  • Cisco CCNA
  • Microsoft Azure Administrator (AZ-104)
  • AWS Cloud Practitioner / Solutions Architect
  • Google IT Support Professional Certificate (free via OSU/OU partnership)

Cybersecurity

One of the highest-demand fields in Oklahoma and nationally. TCC Skills Lab is an accessible accelerated on-ramp in Tulsa.

Key Roles

  • Security Operations Center (SOC) Analyst — Threat monitoring, incident response, SIEM tools
  • Information Security Analyst — Risk assessment, compliance, vulnerability management
  • Cybersecurity Engineer — Network security, firewall/IDS/IPS, zero trust architecture
  • Penetration Tester / Ethical Hacker — Offensive security, red team operations
Starting point for beginners: TCC Skills Lab — free Residency (2–6 months, beginner) and Sprint (6–14 weeks, intermediate) tracks, plus an 8-week advanced Work-Integrated Learning Experience with a $1,000 stipend and certificate/microcredential; free to Oklahoma residents 18+.

A free physical cyber/AI lab in the GEM building at 609 E. Pine St. in Tulsa's Greenwood District, backed by a $10.6 million grant. Partners include SeedAI, Tulsa Innovation Labs, and TEDC; the ASPIRE arm offers free public AI-fluency and workforce workshops.

Skilled Trades (Data Center Construction & Facilities)

Oklahoma's data center construction boom is creating immediate demand for licensed and apprentice-level tradespeople. These are well-paying, union-backed careers.

Key Roles

  • Electrician / Journeyman Electrician — Primary feeder role for data center MEP work
  • HVAC/Mechanical Technician — Cooling systems critical to data center uptime
  • Low-Voltage / Structured Cabling Technician — Fiber, copper, and network infrastructure installation
  • Plumber / Pipefitter — Liquid cooling systems, water distribution
  • Welder / Ironworker — Structural buildout during data center construction phases

Path to Entry

  • Tulsa Electrical JATC — registered apprenticeship; first-period pay $19.19/hr (50% of $38.38/hr journeyman rate), rising to $32.62/hr (85%); $15 application fee and about $650/year for books
  • America's Workforce Academy (Meta/AWA) — CBRE's free Fiber Technician and Construction Ready pathways make participants workforce-ready in four weeks
  • Oklahoma Manufacturing Alliance — Registered ApprenticeshipsNew — employer-facing pathways in occupations such as machine operator, assembler, maintenance technician/mechanic, CNC machinist, material handler, and welder; contact OMA directly because no public learner application window is posted
New · Employer-facing

OMA's official Registered Apprenticeships page is employer-facing and lists no cohort dates or public learner application window. It identifies occupations including Machine Operator, Assembler, Maintenance Technician/Mechanic, CNC Machinist, Material Handler, and Welder.

Upskill Tracks for Working Professionals

Already employed in IT or a related field? These programs are designed to layer new credentials onto existing experience without quitting your job.

UpskillOK — Microcredentials Statewide

Oklahoma's statewide microcredential platform connects working professionals with short-term, skills-based programs at colleges and universities across the state. Programs are stackable and many are available online.

  • Platform: UpskillOK.org
  • Relevant tracks: Computer Networking, Computer Programming, IT Fundamentals, AI Literacy, Cybersecurity
  • Format: Weeks to months; online, hybrid, and in-person options
  • Cost: Varies; some programs are no-cost or under $500
  • State backing: Oklahoma State Regents awarded $150,000 in 2025 grants to accelerate AI-focused microcredential development

TCC Skills Lab — Upskilling Tracks

TCC Skills Lab supports both career changers and upskillers with short, work-aligned pathways at Gradient, 2nd floor, Tulsa.

  • Tracks: Residency (2–6 months, beginner, no cost); Sprint (6–14 weeks, intermediate, no cost); Work-Integrated Learning Experience (8 weeks, advanced, $1,000 stipend plus certificate/microcredential)
  • Fall 2026 focus areas: Cybersecurity, Data Analytics, Artificial Intelligence, UX Design, AI Product Management, Program Management, Project Management, HR Associate
  • Eligibility: Free to Oklahoma residents 18+; funded by the George Kaiser Family Foundation and the Oklahoma Workforce Commission
  • Apply: skillslab.tulsacc.edu/programs

Google Career Certificates — Free via OSU/OU Partnership

Through Google's AI for Education Accelerator, all OSU and OU students, faculty, and staff have no-cost access to:

  • Google Career Certificates (IT Support, Data Analytics, Cybersecurity, Project Management, UX Design)
  • Google AI training courses
  • Google's prior free 12-month student offer closed to new signups January 31, 2026; a new one-year free Google AI Pro student offer launched August 19, 2026
  • Job search support

Also available statewide: The new one-year free Google AI Pro student offer launched August 19, 2026; see Google's student offer page for eligibility and redemption.

  • OSU: news.okstate.edu
  • OSU-Tulsa: Weekly support sessions for Google IT Support Professional Certificate completion

Education & Accelerated Training

America's Workforce Academy (AWA) — Meta

Launched June 2026 | National program with local relevance in Tulsa

Meta launched the America's Workforce Academy in response to massive demand (35,000 applications in 7 days) for skilled trades workers to build its data center infrastructure — including the new AI-optimized data center in East Tulsa that broke ground in April 2026.

What It Is

A nationwide, fast-track training program for skilled trades — zero cost to participants. Meta funds everything: tuition, airfare, lodging, and a daily stipend during training.

Who It's For

  • Veterans, recent graduates, career changers, and new entrants to the trades
  • No prior experience required
  • Open to qualified applicants from all 50 states

What You Get

  • Intensive skills training in electrical work, mechanical systems, plumbing, and related trades
  • National Center for Construction Education and Research (NCCER) certification
  • U.S. Workforce Certificate
  • Guaranteed job offer through a Meta contractor partner upon graduation

Pilot Launch (2026): Louisiana, Ohio, Indiana, and Texas — Oklahoma expansion anticipated as Meta's East Tulsa data center buildout accelerates.

August 2026 update:New Meta's $115 million AWA investment is unchanged, but applications are now open through CBRE. Choose the Fiber Technician or Construction Ready pathway; each is designed to make participants workforce-ready in just four weeks. Meta reports the first Indianapolis cohort graduated and graduations were happening weekly as of August 18, 2026. Oklahoma is still not a 2026 pilot state; pilots remain Louisiana, Ohio, Indiana, and Texas.

Apply / Learn More: CBRE America's Workforce Academy hub · Meta August 18 update
Partnerships contact: awapartnerships@meta.com

K-12: Discover → Explore → Specialize

Oklahoma's career education system gives students a head start long before graduation.

Discover (Elementary School)

Start with curiosity — basic digital citizenship, typing, and logic/coding games. Oklahoma public schools follow state computer science standards that introduce these concepts early. Students and families can use OK Career Guide to explore careers at any age.

Explore (Middle School)

Introduce yourself to computer science or STEM electives: block-based coding, robotics, introductory web design. Use OK Career Guide and OKcollegestart.org to take free interest assessments.

Specialize (High School)

Choose a focus pathway — IT support, networking, web & digital media, programming, or skilled trades — through Oklahoma's IT and Career & Technology Education (CareerTech) cluster plans.

  • Tulsa Tech serves Tulsa County high school students tuition-free. Programs in IT Networking, Cyber Security, Computer Repair, Enterprise Networks, and more are available during the school day or as evening programs.
  • Statewide CareerTech: Oklahoma has 29 technology center districts on 62 campuses serving all 77 counties, tuition-free for in-district high school students. Find your local center at oklahoma.gov/careertech.
  • OKcollegestart.org helps high school students build education plans, search for scholarships, and connect career interests to specific college and CareerTech programs.

After High School: Stackable Pathways to a Career

Oklahoma's postsecondary system is designed to be stackable — you don't have to choose between working and earning credentials.

Step 1 — Free or Low-Cost CareerTech Training

If you didn't complete a CareerTech program in high school, you can enroll as an adult. Tulsa Tech and CareerTech centers statewide offer evening and weekend options. Many are low-cost or eligible for WIOA funding through Oklahoma Works.

Step 2 — Associate (AAS) or Bachelor of Technology (BT)

  • OSU-OKC — AAS in Information Technology (networking, Microsoft systems, IT support tracks)
  • OSUIT — BT in IT with tracks in Cybersecurity, Network Infrastructure, and Software Development
  • Tulsa Community College (TCC) — AAS pathways plus the free TCC Skills Lab

Step 3 — Entry-Level Jobs in Tulsa and Oklahoma

Your combination of CareerTech certifications and AAS/BT credentials positions you for roles like IT Support Specialist, Junior Network Technician, SOC Analyst, Data Center Technician, or Journeyman Electrician.

Veterans & Mid-Career Professionals

If you've served in the military or spent years in another industry, you likely have more transferable skills than you realize. Leadership, troubleshooting under pressure, logistics, communications, and systems thinking all map directly to IT, cybersecurity, and operations roles.

  • GI Bill-eligible programs at Tulsa Tech: Enterprise Network Technologies, Cyber Security/Forensics, and more. Confirm eligibility at benefits.va.gov/GIBILL
  • OSUIT — Multiple BT programs are VA-eligible
  • TCC Skills Lab — free Residency or Sprint training, or an advanced paid Work-Integrated Learning Experience; no GI Bill required
  • America's Workforce Academy (Meta/AWA) — Explicitly designed for veterans entering skilled trades
  • WIOA funding via Oklahoma Works for eligible job seekers

Workforce Initiatives & Ecosystem Partners

Tulsa Innovation Labs (TIL)

The primary workforce and innovation intermediary in Tulsa's tech ecosystem, backed by the George Kaiser Family Foundation. TIL connects employers, educational institutions, and community organizations to align training with industry demand.

  • Tulsa Career Advancement Program (T-CAP) — Upskills new hires and existing workers in autonomous systems specialties. Learn more
  • Workforce Intermediary — Connector between industry and education. Learn more
  • TCC Skills Lab — co-funded by TIL; free Residency and Sprint pathways plus a paid Work-Integrated Learning Experience
  • Doctoral Fellowship in Cyber — Advanced research pipeline
  • Coding Academy — Early-stage software development pipeline

tulsainnovationlabs.com

Oklahoma Works

The state's primary workforce system — connects job seekers to training programs, apprenticeships, and upskilling opportunities. Coordinates with CareerTech, TCC, Tulsa Tech, and employer-sponsored initiatives.

oklahomaworks.gov

Retrain Tulsa

A workforce partner providing wraparound services for adult learners returning to education or transitioning careers — transportation, childcare navigation, coaching, and referral support. It also offers free self-paced TCC + Coursera certificates in IT support, cybersecurity, data analytics, software/web development, project management, and cloud/AI. Active partner with TCC Skills Lab.

SkillStorm

Provides an optional apprenticeship pathway for TCC Skills Lab graduates — hands-on employer-based experience to bridge from training to full-time employment.

Upskill Oklahoma

Oklahoma State Regents' statewide microcredential platform. Aggregates short-term, stackable credential programs from colleges and universities across the state. Focuses on employer-aligned skills for the current and future workforce.

upskillok.org

New · Employer-facing
Oklahoma Talent Accelerator (OKTA) Grant New

An OESC + Guild program backed by an approximately $6 million U.S. DOL grant, OKTA reimburses employers up to 80% of approved training costs (up to $12,500 per employee and $500,000 per employer). Training is free to the worker when an employer participates. Eligible industries: Aerospace & Defense, Advanced Manufacturing, AI/Data Center Infrastructure, and Healthcare (added July 30, 2026).

A new employer- and community-facing initiative rather than an individual training program — but worth tracking as a networking and talent-pipeline touchpoint, since it puts OUPI cybersecurity students directly in front of local organizations.

What it is: Free, faculty-supervised cybersecurity risk assessments for small manufacturers, nonprofits, and tribal businesses in the Tulsa/Northeastern Oklahoma region. Students conduct real client engagements under faculty supervision.

Why it's relevant: Builds OUPI's applied cybersecurity talent pipeline, and could be a referral resource for clients or contacts who need low-cost cyber risk help.
